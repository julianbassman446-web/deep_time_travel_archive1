Open-Ended Research, Macroengineering, and Alternative Approaches

This project is presented as a proposal for investigation, not as a claim that backward time travel has already been achieved.

Future scientists, physicists, engineers, philosophers, historians, and interdisciplinary research teams are invited to evaluate and develop the ideas in this archive. Research should not be limited to the technologies, assumptions, or theoretical frameworks available when this proposal was written.

As future capabilities advance, researchers should consider macroengineering, large-scale energy systems, new physical theories, alternative mechanisms, computational models, and other scientifically or conceptually relevant approaches. They should investigate both promising possibilities and unsuccessful approaches, documenting the evidence and lessons obtained from each.

The absence of a demonstrated mechanism today should not be confused with proof that every conceivable future approach is impossible. Conversely, a mathematically imaginable or technologically proposed mechanism should not be treated as physically achievable without adequate evidence.

Research should continue for as long as meaningful questions, testable hypotheses, or credible new approaches remain. Each generation of researchers should preserve its findings and make the results available to those who follow.

The long-term objective is to investigate whether backward time travel, communication with the past, or another physically meaningful form of temporal access could ever become possible, including whether a safe means of reaching October 16, 2026, could exist.

The personal motivation behind this project is the possibility of a life do-over: revisiting important decisions, gaining closure, and pursuing goals that matter. That motivation should be preserved as part of the historical record, while the feasibility of the proposed technology remains an open research question.
